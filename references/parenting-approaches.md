# Parenting And Early-Education Approaches

Read this file only when the user names a method, asks which philosophy fits, or describes a recognizable method rule. Use `care-baselines.md` and current safety evidence as the floor.

## How To Use A Method Card

1. Identify whether the user wants explanation, comparison, selective use, or full-method implementation.
2. State what the method itself teaches from its original source.
3. Separate evidence-aligned components, practical heuristics, and values or preferences.
4. Check the proposed action against current safety, feeding, sleep, development, and family constraints.
5. Recommend the smallest useful component. Do not require ideological consistency.

Do not ask `which method is best` in the abstract. Compare methods against the family's actual goal, the baby's current signals and ability, safety constraints, workload, and reversibility.

## Name Disambiguation

- **AAP** normally means the American Academy of Pediatrics, a professional pediatric organization.
- **AP** in parenting discussions often means Attachment Parenting, a parenting philosophy.
- Ask or infer from context when an abbreviation is ambiguous; never substitute one for the other.

## Montessori 0-3

**Identity and original source:** Association Montessori Internationale (AMI) [Montessori 0-3](https://montessori-ami.org/node/2174).

**Core method claims:** observation of the child; a prepared, orderly, accessible environment; age-appropriate opportunities for movement, language, participation, and independence; different 0-3 environments such as Nido and Infant Community.

**Useful components:** place a few reachable materials in a safe area; adapt furniture and tasks to current ability; allow repetition and participation in daily care; observe before adding help or stimulation.

**Values or method-specific choices:** aesthetic simplicity, material selection, sequencing, adult role, and the amount of direct demonstration are partly pedagogical choices. They should be adapted to the home rather than treated as universal clinical requirements.

**Evidence boundary:** the Montessori label or an AMI description establishes the method's identity, not that a product, toy, shelf layout, course, or early milestone claim has proven superiority. Evaluate specific developmental claims separately.

**Red lines:** no unsafe small parts, climbing setup, furniture, sleep setup, forced posture, or activity beyond current ability. `Montessori` marketing does not override product-safety checks.

**Best use:** environment design, independent play opportunities, practical-life participation, and reducing unnecessary adult interruption.

**Not sufficient for:** diagnosis, treating delay, guaranteeing concentration or intelligence, or deciding that a particular branded item is necessary.

## RIE / Pikler

**Identity and original sources:** [RIE Educaring](https://rie.org/educaring-approach/) and [Pikler pedagogical guide](https://resources.pikler.hu/pedagogical-guide).

**Core method claims:** respectful, stable relationships; active participation during care; careful observation; safe environments; uninterrupted self-initiated play; freedom of movement; minimal necessary help; predictable care.

**Useful components:** tell the baby what is about to happen; invite participation during feeding, dressing, bathing, and diapering; pause to observe before intervening; provide floor time and a safe movement environment.

**Values or method-specific choices:** the degree of adult intervention, emphasis on uninterrupted play, equipment choices, and interpretation of self-initiated development are features of the approach rather than universal medical rules.

**Evidence boundary:** individual principles overlap with responsive caregiving and developmentally appropriate practice. Do not claim that adopting the complete branded approach guarantees attachment, motor, personality, or mental-health outcomes.

**Red lines:** observation is not a reason to delay help for distress, feeding need, pain, danger, or a developmental concern. Freedom of movement still requires a safe setup and appropriate supervision.

**Best use:** care routines, observation, reducing over-helping, floor play, and caregiver-child cooperation.

**Not sufficient for:** medical developmental assessment, unsupervised equipment use, or a rule that adults should never demonstrate or assist.

## Attachment Parenting

**Identity and original source:** Attachment Parenting International [Eight Principles](https://www.attachmentparenting.org/introduction).

**Core method claims:** nurturing connection through preparation, responsive feeding, sensitive response, nurturing touch, safe sleep, consistent care, positive discipline, and family balance.

**Useful components:** take bids for comfort seriously; build predictable caregiver availability; use non-violent, relationship-preserving responses; consider caregiver and family balance.

**Values or method-specific choices:** families may choose different feeding, carrying, soothing, sleep, and proximity practices. No single practice is the only route to a secure relationship.

**Evidence boundary:** Attachment Parenting is not identical to attachment theory, and method adherence cannot diagnose or guarantee secure attachment. Assess each concrete practice with current evidence and family context.

**Red lines:** safe-sleep requirements, caregiver impairment, medical feeding needs, and severe exhaustion override an ideological requirement for proximity or one specific soothing practice.

**Best use:** discussing responsiveness, connection, caregiving consistency, and the user's values around closeness.

**Not sufficient for:** diagnosing attachment, predicting personality, or claiming that sleep arrangement, feeding mode, or babywearing determines attachment quality.

## Tracy Hogg E.A.S.Y. / 程序育儿法

**Identity and original source:** Tracy Hogg and Melinda Blau's E.A.S.Y. method; the [publisher description](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/The-Baby-Whisperer-Solves-All-Your-Problems/Tracy-Hogg/9781439106549) defines the sequence as Eat, Activity, Sleep, and time for You, and describes it as a predictable routine rather than fitting a baby to a clock.

**Core method claims:** use a repeated sequence to make the day more predictable; observe cues while the caregiver provides structure; separate some feeding and sleep associations; preserve caregiver time.

**Useful components:** track the order of events instead of an exact clock; create repeatable transition cues; use the pattern as an observation tool; protect caregiver recovery where possible.

**Values or method-specific choices:** eat-activity-sleep order, the preferred degree of caregiver leadership, settling techniques, and reducing particular sleep associations are practical method choices, not universal developmental requirements.

**Evidence boundary:** the original book or publisher page establishes the method, not the effectiveness of the whole package. Evaluate feeding intervals, night needs, sleep interventions, and age claims separately.

**Red lines:** never delay feeding, medical care, comfort for pain/illness, or necessary sleep solely to preserve the sequence. Do not wake, stretch wake time, remove feeds, or apply settling steps from a generic age table without the child's current context.

**Best use:** families seeking flexible structure, sequence-based observation, and lower coordination load.

**Not sufficient for:** fixed three-hour schedules, predicting when a baby should sleep through the night, proving self-soothing ability, or treating feeding/sleep disorders.
