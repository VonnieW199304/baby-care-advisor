# Knowledge Boundaries

Use these distinctions to prevent a plausible answer from becoming an unsupported diagnosis or rigid parenting rule.

## Behavior And Causes

- Separate observation, pattern, possible mechanism, risk signal, screening result, and diagnosis.
- For a simple everyday question, name only the mechanism that changes the action. Do not force a main/secondary cause table.
- For a genuinely complex pattern, rank `most likely main factor`, `possible secondary factor`, and `trigger or maintaining factor` from the supplied facts. Do not assign numeric weights unless data support them.
- Do not rank a newly acquired developmental skill, recent routine change, or other temporally adjacent event from timing alone. Main-factor ranking needs a stable temporal relationship, repeated covariation, or an observed response when that factor changes; otherwise keep it as an unranked possible contributor and do not use priority synonyms. Do not infer skill rehearsal, depleted motor drive, separation anxiety, teething, or a new sleep association unless the user's observations support that mechanism.
- Ordinary baby behavior is not evidence of manipulation, spoiled character, giftedness, attachment disorder, or future personality.

## Development

- A milestone age is a surveillance prompt, not a deadline shared by every child.
- A checklist cannot diagnose delay. Concern becomes stronger with loss of acquired skills, persistent cross-setting difficulty, multiple related signals, or functional impact.
- Corrected age matters for children born preterm when appropriate, but the skill must not invent it.
- One still photo cannot establish muscle tone, symmetry in motion, a recurring posture, pain, or developmental level.

## Growth

- Use the chart matching age, sex, measurement type, and reference system.
- Trend across reliable measurements is usually more informative than one percentile.
- Measurement error, illness, prematurity, and feeding context can affect interpretation.
- A percentile is descriptive, not a diagnosis or target to chase.

## Sleep

- Separate safe-sleep requirements from ordinary sleep-pattern troubleshooting.
- Do not prescribe a fixed timetable only from age averages. Use the baby's current pattern, sleep opportunity, feeding, comfort, and daytime context.
- A waking pattern can have several contributors. Give a reversible test and an observation window instead of claiming one cause.
- Do not let a sleep-training discussion weaken immediate safe-sleep guidance.

## Feeding

- Separate: whether a food category is age-appropriate, whether the exact packaged product is suitable, and whether the preparation/texture is safe.
- Population or institution feeding guidance is not an exact intake prescription for one child.
- Legal permission for an ingredient does not automatically make it preferable; preference for a simpler label does not automatically make other legal formulations unsafe.
- Allergy, choking, swallowing, poor growth, special formula, and therapeutic diet questions may require professional assessment.

## Medical Boundary

- The skill performs triage and decision support, not diagnosis.
- Medication advice requires the exact active ingredient, formulation, concentration, route, indication, child weight when relevant, contraindications, and approved instructions. If any decisive item is missing, do not calculate a dose.
- A current official page can establish a rule or label, but not whether this child personally has the condition or should receive treatment.

## Action Shape

- Give `do now` and `longer-term adjustment` only when they are materially different. Do not force both sections into every answer.
- Prefer one low-risk experiment with a clear observation window over a long routine overhaul.
- State escalation conditions only when relevant to the described risk; do not append a generic warning catalogue.

## Parenting Methods And Early-Education Philosophies

- A named method is a bundle of principles, heuristics, values, and sometimes factual or causal claims. Evaluate those components separately.
- Original method material is authoritative for what the method says, not for whether it works, is safe, or is superior.
- Similarity between a method principle and evidence-informed responsive care does not validate every part of the branded method.
- Do not infer attachment quality, future personality, intelligence, independence, or developmental acceleration from method adherence.
- When methods conflict, safety, adequate feeding and sleep opportunity, current child signals, developmental fit, and caregiver sustainability outrank fidelity.
- Combine compatible components when useful, but describe the result as a tailored plan rather than falsely attributing it to one method.
